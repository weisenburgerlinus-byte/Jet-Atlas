Dassault Rafale.

Twin-engine Multirole fighter

The Rafale is designed to operate from both land bases and aircraft carriers. Its mission include air defense, reconnaissance and surface attack.

Weapons & payload

What can the Rafale carry?

The Rafale has 14 external store stations, or 13 on the carrier-based Rafale M. Its exact weapon fit depends on the aircraft variant, operator and mission.

External capacity

Up to 9.5 tonnes

Five stations can carry heavy stores or drop tanks. The built-in 30 mm cannon is separate from the external load.

  • METEOR / MICA Air-to-air missiles
  • AASM HAMMER / SCALP Air-to-ground weapons
  • AM39 EXOCET Air-to-sea missile

These are examples of weapons cleared for the Rafale, not one fixed loadout. The 14 stations and external-load figure describe carrying capacity; they do not mean every aircraft carries the same number or combination of weapons.

01 METEOR Air-to-air missile

What it is: A long-range air-to-air missile powered by a solid-fuel ramjet.

How it works: An active radar seeker supports guidance near the end of flight. A data link can provide updates while the missile is in flight.

Guidance
Inertial navigation, data link and active radar
Propulsion
Solid-fuel ramjet
Warhead
Blast-fragmentation
02 MICA Air-to-air missile

What it is: An air-to-air missile available with infrared or active radar seeker options.

How it works: The chosen seeker tracks the target, while the missile's onboard guidance system controls its flight after release.

Guidance
Infrared or active radar
Propulsion
Solid-propellant rocket motor
Role
Short- and beyond-visual-range air combat
03 AASM HAMMER Guided air-to-ground weapon

What it is: A modular weapon that combines a compatible bomb body with guidance and range-extension kits.

How it works: Depending on the kit, it uses inertial and satellite navigation, infrared imaging, or laser guidance to adjust its flight.

Guidance
INS/GPS, infrared imaging or laser
Construction
Bomb body, guidance kit and range kit
04 SCALP Stand-off cruise missile

What it is: A long-range, air-launched cruise missile.

How it works: Its navigation combines inertial and satellite data with terrain reference. An imaging infrared seeker compares the target area with stored imagery during the final phase.

Navigation
Inertial, GPS and terrain reference
Final guidance
Imaging infrared seeker
05 AM39 EXOCET Air-launched anti-ship missile

What it is: The air-launched member of the Exocet anti-ship missile family.

How it works: The Exocet family uses active radar homing and a low-level sea-skimming flight profile.

Guidance
Active radar homing
Role
Air-to-sea
At a glance

Three versions, one family

The Rafale is available in three versions. They share a common design, with different for land-based and carrier operations.

RAFALE C Single-seat version for land bases.
RAFALE B Two-seat version for land bases.
RAFALE M Single-seat version for aircraft carriers.
Design & role

Built for different missions

Dassault describes the Rafale as an “omnirole“ aircraft. It can perform missions such as air defense, close air support, reconnaissance and anti-ship strikes.

The French Navy began operating its first Rafale squadron in 2004. The French Air Force followed in 2006.
