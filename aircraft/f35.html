Weapons & payload

What can the F-35 carry?

Weapon fit varies by aircraft variant, operator and mission. This section shows one document internal F-35 configuration.

Example internal load F-35

Two missiles and two guided bombs

Lockheed Martin lists this as one standard internal configuration. The F-35A also has an internal 25 mm cannon.

  • 2 x AIM-120C/D AMRAAM Air-to-air missiles
  • 2 x GBU-31 JDAM Guided air-to-surface bombs

This is an example F-35A internal load, not a universal loadout or the maximum payload. Other F-35 variants and operators use different approved configurations.

01 AIM-120C/D AMRAAM Air-to-air missile

What is it: A powered, radar-guided air-to-air missile.

How it works: It uses inertial guidance and can receive updates from the aircraft during flight. Near the end of its flight, its own active radar seeker guides it toward the target.

Guidance
Inertial, data-link updates and active radar
Propulsion
Solid-fuel rocket motor
Warhead
Blast-fragmentation
02 GBU-31 JDAM Guided bomb

What it is: A conventional free-fall bomb fitted with a guidance and control tail kit.

How it works: GPS-aided inertial navigation and the tail's control surfaces adjust its path after release. Unlike a missile, it has no powered motor.

Guidance
GPS-aided inertial navigation
Type
2,000-pound guided bomb

At a glance

Three ways to take off

The variants share a common design but are build for different takeoff and landing methods. The family discovers conventional runways, short with vertical landings and aircraft carrier operations.

F-35A Conventional takeoff and landing from runways.
F-35B Short takeoff and vertical landing.
F-35C Designed to operate from large aircraft carriers.
Design & role

A connected aircraft

The F-35 is designed as a multirole aircraft. Alongside its combat role, the manufacturer's description emphases gathering, processing and sharing information.

The F-35A made its first flight on December 15, 2006. The three variants are part of the Lightning II family.
