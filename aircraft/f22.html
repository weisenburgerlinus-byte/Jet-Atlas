Weapons & payload

What can the F-22 carry?

This section shows one document internal F-22A air-to-air configuration. Other mission loads differ.

Example air-to-air load F-22A

Eight air-to-air missiles

The F-22A also has an internal 20 mm cannon; it is not included in this missile count.

  • 6 x AIM-120 AMRAAM Air-to-air missiles
  • 2 x AIM-9 Sidewinder Air-to-air missiles

This is the published air-to-air configuration, not the only F-22 loadout. The aircraft also has document air-to-ground configuration.

01 AIM-120 AMRAAMAir-to-air missile

What it is: A powered, radar-guided air to-air missile.

How it works: It uses inertial guidance and can receive updates from the aircraft during flight. Near the end of its flight, its own active radar seeker guides it toward the target.

Guidance
Inertial, data-link updates and active radar
Propulsion
Solid-fuel rocket motor
Warhead
Blast-fragmentation
02 AIM-9 Sidewinder Air-to-air missile

What it is: A short-range, infrared-guided air-to-air missile.

How it works: Its infrared seeker detects heat from an aircraft. Control surfaces steer the missile during-flight. Details vary between Sidewinder versions.

Guidance
Infrared homing
Propulsion
Solid-propellant rocket motor
Warhead
High-explosive

At a glance

Built for air superiority

The F-22 Raptor is a single-seat air-superiority aircraft. Lockheed Martin describes it as combining low observability, speed, maneuverability and situational awareness.

Key features

technology working together

Its design combines low observability, supercruise, high maneuverability, information fusion and improved maintainability. The first flight took place on 7 September, 1997 and the aircraft became operational in 2005.

These features work together to collect and combine information, helping the pilot maintain awareness of the situation.
